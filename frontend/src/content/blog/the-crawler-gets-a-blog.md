Why No IPv6 has crawled the top million domains every day for years and kept the
commentary to one-liners: [a leaderboard](/domains), [a changelog](/changelog),
a rating out of four stars. Some findings don't fit in a table cell. This is
where they go.

## What to expect

- **Adoption digests.** What moved, what didn't. Mostly what didn't.
- **Write-ups when a big name changes.** A top-100 domain publishing its first
  AAAA record is news. A top-100 domain quietly deleting one is also news, and
  considerably funnier.
- **Country deep dives.** Who leads, who trails, and which national averages
  are carried by a single determined ISP.
- **Methodology notes.** When the crawler learns a new trick, we'll explain
  what it checks and why, so the numbers stay arguable on the merits.

No release-note filler. If a post exists, the data said something.

## The numbers are frozen on purpose

Stats in a post are a snapshot from its publish date, and they stay that way.
A sentence someone cites should still be true of the day it was written. The
live figures keep moving on [the metrics page](/metrics), and every post links
back to the pages and the [API](https://api.whynoipv6.com/docs) it was drawn
from, so checking our work is one click.

## Subscribe

There's an [RSS feed](/blog/rss.xml). No newsletter, no popup. This site
exists to shame people for skipping an open standard; it is not going to skip
the one for syndication.
